<i> Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: ACL 2026, pages 6632–6655  ©2026 Association for Computational Linguistics.</i> 
<i> The paper investigates the quality and gender representation of machine translation datasets for three Ethiopian languages: Afan Oromo, Amharic, and Tigrinya. Using automated analyses and human evaluation, the authors examine topics, person names, grammatical gender, and gender-related stereotypes in training and benchmark datasets. The findings reveal that datasets are heavily skewed toward male representation, with female names accounting for less than 20% in the evaluated datasets, alongside gender stereotypes and harmful depictions of women. The study also identifies mismatches between training and benchmark data domains and cases of code-mixing or text in other languages. It emphasizes that collecting more data does not necessarily improve quality and recommends more careful dataset curation, quality control, and early evaluation of gender bias to support fairer and more reliable language technologies for low-resource African languages.</i>.'
